    About our data

    Which numbers are measured, which are modelled, and where each one comes from.

    Wholesale electricity price (USEP) Live, provisional

    The Uniform Singapore Energy Price is set for every half-hour of the day. Wattly reads it from the free NEMS API, an unofficial community service with no uptime guarantee. When it is down, Wattly keeps showing the last price it saved and marks it as stale.

    Live prices are provisional. The Energy Market Company publishes final prices a few business days later (EMC NEMS prices). USEP is a wholesale signal, not your full tariff.

    Past prices Official, final

    To train and check the price forecast, Wattly also uses EMC's official final USEP history, downloaded as CSV from the market data download on EMC's NEMS Prices page. It covers 1 June 2025 to 30 September 2026 with no missing half-hours. These are final prices, so they are not exactly what was known live at the time.

    Other charges on the bill Published rates

    A price-linked bill is more than the wholesale price. For "Spent so far" and realised savings, Wattly adds SP Group's published charges for a low-tension household buying at the wholesale electricity price. The rates below exclude GST.

    Price forecast Estimate

    The Forecast page estimates USEP for the next 24 half-hours (12 hours). It comes from a model trained on the past prices above, with Singapore public holidays from the Ministry of Manpower. When the model isn't available, Wattly falls back to a simple baseline built from past prices and never labels it as AI. Forecasts can be wrong, especially during price spikes.

    A half-hour is flagged as a likely spike when its forecast is well above the typical price for that time over the last 28 days. With too little history, nothing is flagged.

    Suggestions can also use a demo forecast: the real prices of 26 September 2026 replayed by time of day. Every suggestion built from it is labelled "Demo forecast".

    Your household's electricity use Modelled, not metered

    There is no public way to read a home's live smart-meter data, so Wattly models it:

    • We start from EMA's average monthly use for your home type (table below).
    • We subtract the appliances you listed, because we place those at their own times. EV charging is not in EMA's averages, so it is added on top and shown separately.
    • The rest is spread across the day with a typical Singapore pattern: aircon at night, a morning bump, a quiet weekday afternoon and an evening peak. Weekends use 10% more per day. This daily pattern is our assumption, not published data.
    • An appliance run three times a week counts as 3/7 of a run every day, so the numbers are averages.

    Over a whole month, the base load plus your appliances adds up exactly to EMA's figure.

    Appliances You enter them

    Power is in kilowatts (how hard an appliance draws) and run time in minutes. Energy per run is power × hours, in kilowatt-hours. Your cost is energy × price.

    Weather Live forecast

    Daily temperature forecasts come from the National Environment Agency through data.gov.sg. A hot-day hint is a temperature signal, not a price prediction.

    Wattly also saves NEA's two-hour area forecasts next to each price, so weather and prices can be studied together.

    Spending and budget Modelled

    Spent so far this month adds up every finished half-hour since the 1st: your modelled use × that half-hour's market price, plus the charges above and GST. A half-hour with no saved price is left out and counted, never guessed. It is not your bill: use is modelled, not metered, and the vesting charge is missing.

    Projected this month is our assumption: spent so far + your average daily spend over the last 7 full days × the days left. Days with any missing price are left out of that average. If there are none left, Wattly shows no projection instead of guessing.

    Households in the admin view Simulated

    Apart from a handful of demo homes, the households in the admin view are generated, not real people. Their home-type mix roughly follows Singapore's housing stock and 70% are on price-linked plans, both of which are demo assumptions. They are always labelled "simulated" and cannot be used to log in.

    Savings Assumption

    Because Wattly cannot see your meter, an accepted suggestion is treated as followed: we assume you ran the appliance at the time you chose instead of your usual time.

    • Estimated saving is what the forecast predicted, using the wholesale price only.
    • Saved replaces it once both runs have finished and every half-hour they cover has an actual price. It is the cost at your usual time minus the cost at the time you chose, at actual prices plus the per-kWh charges and GST. It can be negative if the chosen time turned out dearer. On a fixed-rate plan it is always $0.
    • The run's power and length are saved when you accept, so editing the appliance later doesn't change past savings.
    • How sure counts how many of the last 28 days, at actual prices, the suggested time really was cheaper than your usual time. With fewer than 14 days of prices it says there isn't enough history yet.

    All sources

    • Live USEP: NEMS API (unofficial)
    • Final USEP and history: EMC NEMS Prices
    • Average household use: MSE reply to a parliamentary question (EMA data)
    • Bill charges: SP Group fact sheet (1 July 2026) and SP PowerAssets rate schedule
    • Public holidays: Ministry of Manpower
    • Daily weather: NEA on data.gov.sg
    • Two-hour weather: NEA two-hour forecast API
